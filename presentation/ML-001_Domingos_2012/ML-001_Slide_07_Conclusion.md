## Speaker notes (not displayed on the slide)

To conclude, Domingos's article demonstrates that successful machine learning requires much more than selecting a powerful algorithm.

A model may achieve excellent training accuracy but fail on unseen data. A feature may appear irrelevant individually but become valuable when combined with another feature. A statistical association may help us make predictions without explaining the underlying cause.

These examples demonstrate why researchers must carefully consider how they represent data, train models, evaluate performance, and interpret their results.

Domingos also emphasizes that practical knowledge complements the formal study of machine learning rather than replacing it.

My main takeaway from this article is that we should not simply ask whether a machine learning model works. We should also ask why it works, under what conditions it works, and how much confidence we can place in its results.
